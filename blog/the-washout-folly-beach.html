The Washout

Folly Island's most famous surf break: how it formed, when it works, and what to know before you paddle out.

The Washout is the stretch of East Ashley Avenue on the northeast end of Folly Island where Hurricane Hugo flattened the dunes, the road and a full row of beach houses in 1989. With nothing left standing between the ocean and the Folly River, wind now moves straight across the island — and that open corridor is what local surfers credit for the most consistent waves on the South Carolina coast. It sits a few minutes up the road from Center Street and the rest of Folly Beach, SC.

How Hugo Built South Carolina's Best Break

Before 1989 this was a typical block of oceanfront homes. Hugo's surge wiped it clean, and the city never rebuilt the lots that washed out. A line of short rock groins built to hold the sand now breaks the beach into a series of peaks, which spreads surfers out and gives the spot more than one takeoff zone. The Folly Beach surf spot guide and Surfline's regional notes both rank it as the top break on the island.

Conditions That Make It Work

The Washout is at its best from mid to high tide, when north or northwest winds groom the faces. Northeast swells and the hurricane swells of late summer and fall produce the most power; a southeast swell paired with a northwest breeze is the classic combination locals wait for. Flat days are common, so check the Surfline Washout report or our roundup of Folly Beach surf cams before making the drive.

Hazards and Lineup Etiquette

Those groins are the main hazard — they sit roughly every hundred yards and can be partly underwater at higher tides, so note where they are before paddling out. The longshore current is strong enough to carry you a couple hundred yards down the beach in a short session. On a good swell the lineup gets crowded and competitive: wait your turn, don't drop in, and give the regulars room. City rules require a leash for every surfer, everywhere on the island. This is not a beginner break.

Where Newer Surfers Should Paddle Out

10th Street East is a popular alternative that breaks smaller on northeast swells and holds a south swell nicely. The Folly Beach Pier area has a wide beach and fewer crowds, but surfing is always banned within 200 feet of the pier. From May 15 to September 15, surfing is also off-limits between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m. in the swimming zone from 2nd Street East to 3rd Street West, per the City of Folly Beach beach rules. If you are just starting out, book a session with one of the schools listed at Folly Beach Surf Lessons and save the Washout for later.

Parking, Gear and Food After the Session

On a good swell, parking near the Washout disappears early. Park legally along East Ashley with all four tires off the road and follow the posted parking rules; tickets are common. For rentals, wax and local intel, McKevlin's Surf Shop at 8 Center Street and Ocean Surf Shop on Center Street both know what the break is doing that morning.

The post-surf ritual is food on Center Street. Lost Dog Cafe is the breakfast standby, Taco Boy at 106 East Ashley Avenue handles lunch, and Rita's Seaside Grille at 2 Center Street has a patio by the pier for a late afternoon drink. Our dining guide has the rest.

Not here to surf? The Washout is still one of the best places on the island to watch a good swell roll in. Find more to do on the Folly Beach Now homepage or start a trip plan with our vacation planner.

At a Glance

  • Location: East Ashley Ave, northeast end of the island
  • Best tide: mid to high
  • Best wind: N / NW
  • Best swell: NE and hurricane swells
  • Skill level: intermediate and up
  • Leash: required island-wide
