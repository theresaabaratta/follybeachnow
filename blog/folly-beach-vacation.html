Planning a Folly Beach Vacation

Where to stay, where to eat, what to do and the island rules to know before you arrive.

Folly Island sits about 12 miles south of downtown Charleston — close enough for a dinner reservation on King Street, far enough that the island still feels like a laid-back beach town. Here's how to plan a stay around the parts that matter: where to sleep, where to eat, what to do and which rules can earn you a ticket. For daily updates and everything else, start at the Folly Beach in Charleston homepage.

Choosing Between the Beach Side and the River Side

Oceanfront and front-beach rentals put you steps from the sand and the action around the pier. The river side, along the Folly River and the marsh, trades ocean views for quieter streets, docks and some of the best sunsets in the Lowcountry. Most visitors book houses or condos through Vacasa, Vrbo or Airbnb; legitimate listings show a City of Folly Beach short-term rental registration number. If you'd rather have a hotel, Tides Folly Beach sits right at the foot of Center Street next to the pier.

On a budget or traveling with an RV, the campgrounds in our camping guide are a short drive from the island.

Center Street Is the Heart of the Island

Nearly everything happens within a few blocks of Center Street, so you can park once and walk. Rita's Seaside Grille at 2 Center Street serves breakfast through dinner on a patio with live music, and BLU, the oceanfront restaurant at Tides at 1 Center Street, has the best water views on the strip. Taco Boy, a block off Center Street at 106 East Ashley Avenue, is the go-to for tacos and margaritas, and Folly Beach Crab Shack at 26 Center Street is the go-to for steamed seafood buckets. Mornings belong to Lost Dog Cafe, and on the way back toward Charleston, Bowens Island Restaurant serves roasted oysters on the marsh. Our full Folly dining guide has more.

Things to Do Beyond Beach Days

The Folly Beach Pier stretches more than 1,000 feet into the Atlantic and is the island's best spot for sunrise and fishing (no fishing after sunset). At the far northeast end of East Ashley Avenue, Lighthouse Inlet Heritage Preserve has the best land view of the Morris Island Lighthouse; note that pets are not allowed and swimming is off-limits there. Families tend to stick to Folly Beach County Park on the west end, which has restrooms and a calmer setup.

Surfers should read our guide to the Washout, the island's most famous break, and check the live surf cams each morning. Kayak and paddleboard rentals are easy to find on the river side, and the island sits on the Atlantic Flyway, so herons, egrets and osprey are everywhere. Check the events page for festivals and live music during your dates, and if you're coming with friends, our girls' weekend guide has a group itinerary.

Beach Rules Worth Knowing Before You Pack

Folly enforces its beach rules, and fines can ruin a day. Alcohol, glass and smoking are banned on the beach and beach accesses, and plastic bags, balloons and Styrofoam aren't allowed either. Dogs are off the sand from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. between May 1 and September 30, leashed at other times and never allowed on the pier. Loggerhead turtles nest from May through October, so keep lights off the beach at night and fill in any holes you dig. Golf carts need both SC DMV and City of Folly Beach stickers and can't be driven at night or in the rain.

Timing the Trip

Summer is peak season, with full rentals, packed parking and lifeguards on duty. September and October are many locals' favorite months: the water is still warm, crowds thin out and hurricane swells bring the best surf of the year. Winter is quiet and much cheaper, and in November and December the holiday light show at nearby James Island County Park makes an easy evening outing.

Keep this handy: bookmark the Folly Beach Now homepage for current events, dining picks and island news while you're in town.

At a Glance

  • Distance: ~12 miles south of downtown Charleston
  • Stay: front beach, river side or Tides hotel
  • Eat: Center Street, walkable from the pier
  • See: the pier, Morris Island Lighthouse, the Washout
  • Best value: September–October
